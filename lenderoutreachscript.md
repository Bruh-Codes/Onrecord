# Lender Outreach — Intro Message & Meeting Script
**For the 2-week validation experiment. Purpose: honest research, not sales.**

---

## Part 1 — The cold/outreach message (copy-paste templates)

### Version A — LinkedIn / WhatsApp / Email (text)
> Hello [Name], I hope you're well.
>
> I'm [name], a [student / founder] working on a research project with [university / program]. We're exploring whether Ghanaian lenders would benefit from a tool that reads an SME's raw records (MoMo statements, receipts, handwritten books) and turns them into a **balance-checked, fraud-screened financial file** — so loan officers don't have to re-key six months of statements by hand.
>
> We are **not selling anything** — we're at the very early stage and genuinely want your honest opinion, even if it's brutally negative. Would you be open to a 20-minute call or meeting this week or next? Your insight would be extremely valuable to us.
>
> Best regards,
> [Name]

**Notes on the messaging:**
- Lead with the **lender's cost/efficiency pain** (re-keying), not the borrower's readiness.
- Explicitly say "not selling, want honest feedback, even negative" — this lowers the guard and gets truer answers.
- Name the **fraud-screening** explicitly (it's your strongest asset and the least-copyable).
- Don't use the word "score" in the intro (regulatory + triggers skepticism). That's a T6 conversation, not a first hook.

### Version B — If you have a warm intro (personalized)
> Hi [Name], [mutual contact] suggested I reach out. I'm exploring a concept that could save your officers significant time on SME credit files — automatically reading and **balance-verifying** their transaction records and **flagging doctored or duplicate statements**.
>
> We're early-stage and just looking for an honest 20 minutes of your expert read on whether this would actually help your team. Happy to share a sample file so it's concrete, not abstract. Would that work?

## Part 2 — The 20–30 minute meeting script

### Step 1 — Framing (2 min, be transparent)
> "Thank you for the time. To be upfront: we're evaluating a concept, not trying to sell you anything today. You can be completely honest — even if you think this is a bad idea, that's exactly what we want to hear. Your feedback tells us whether to build this at all."

**Why:** Explicitly invite brutal honesty. If they say something negative, probe it, don't defend it. This is research, not a pitch.

### Step 2 — Show the mock verified file (5 min)
> "Here's a sample of what we'd produce for a trader who uploads their MoMo records and receipts. [Walk through: clean ledger, every number traced to a source, balanced to the pesewa, verification report.] The point isn't that the AI is smart — it's that **every figure can be audited back to a document, and the ledger adds up.** Nothing is invented."

**Watch for:** Do they lean in and touch the numbers, or glance and move on? Do they ask "how did you get this" (good) or dismiss it as "just another spreadsheet" (bad)?

### Step 3 — Show the fraud case (5 min) ← the persuasive core
> "Now the part we think matters most to your team. [Show the doctored-looking statement + the flag.] We detect duplicate files submitted for different applicants, 'bank statements' actually made in Microsoft Word, and edited photos. Anything suspicious gets flagged for a human — it's **never silently accepted.** We're on your side against fraud, not just helping borrowers make nicer files."

**Watch for:** This is where trust is won or lost. If they light up here, T4 is your strongest signal. If they say "our team/all this is our value," capture that.

### Step 4 — Work through the T-questions (10 min)
Use the scorecard in `lendervalidationexperiment.md` §3. Ask explicitly, get a **yes/no plus the reason**:

- **T1:** "Off the record — would your officers ever *use* a file from a third-party tool like this as useful input?"
- **T2:** "Do you accept a MoMo-only file, or do you always need bank statements?"
- **T3:** "Would balance-checked, provenance-backed numbers genuinely cut your verification time?"
- **T4:** "Is fraud-screening like this something you'd value or pay for?"
- **T5:** "If yes — would you pay a per-file fee, or a seat subscription? Roughly what could that be worth?"
- **T6:** "What's your read on a 'credit readiness score' — useful, risky, or a regulatory red flag?" (Get their *real* institutional take.)
- **T8 (the hard truth):** "Would your team accept this file as-is, or still demand original statements from the source?"
- **T9:** "If this existed and was solid, would you seriously consider routing ~20 SMEs through a real pilot?"

### Step 5 — Probe the two hard truths (don't skip)
1. **Override trust:** "If our file flagged a transaction as suspect, would your team trust that, or investigate from scratch anyway?"
2. **Liability:** "Real talk — if a file we processed turned out to be fraudulent, who would you expect to own that risk — us or you?"

### Step 6 — Close (1 min)
> "This has been genuinely useful — thank you. Two quick asks: First, is there anyone else in your institution or network you'd recommend we speak to? Second, if this existed in ~[timeframe], would you be open to us circling back? We'll make sure to share our findings — it's only fair since you helped shape them."

**Why:** A concrete forward signal ("yes, circle back" or "here's the right person") is stronger than "sounds interesting." And offering to share findings builds reciprocity + reputation.

## Part 3 — What NOT to do in these meetings

1. **Don't sell.** The moment it smells like a pitch, the honest answers stop. Stay in "research" mode the whole time.
2. **Don't defend your idea.** If they say it's weak, write it down and move on. Arguing poisons the signal.
3. **Don't overclaim.** Don't say "AI reads everything perfectly" — say "every number traces to a source and the ledger balances." Under-promise.
4. **Don't show the "readiness score" prominently.** It's part of the T6 conversation, not the headline. Lead with verification + fraud-screening.
5. **Don't use a registered-name brand** that implies you're an existing regulated entity. Keep it clearly "research/concept."

---

## Part 4 — Same-day logging (the discipline)

Within **2 hours** of each meeting, log into the scorecard (`lendervalidationexperiment.md` §7 for the green/yellow/red signals):

- T1–T9 answers (yes/no + 1-line reason)
- Any **specific pain quote** ("we spend a day per file re-keying") — these are gold for the final pitch
- Any **objection verbatim** (these are what we must design around)
- A 1-line overall read: green / yellow / red

**Do not trust memory. Same-day, every time.** The quality of the go/no-go decision depends entirely on capturing these faithfully.

## Part 5 — The 2-week execution cheat-sheet

| Day | Action |
|---|---|
| D1–2 | Build the 2 mock files (start with `mockfraudcase.md` structure) |
| D3–4 | Fill the target list (8–10) with this script's messaging |
| D5–8 | Run the 5+ meetings, log same-day |
| D9 | Run the fraud-screening *fake door* off any contact list |
| D10–12 | Consolidate scorecard, write go/no-go read |
| D13–14 | Team decision: PROCEED / ADJUST / PIVOT |

**Remember the goal:** We are not looking for validation — we are looking for the **truth**, especially the uncomfortable kind. A lender who says "we'd trust the file but never pay for it" is a YELLOW signal worth more than five polite "great idea"s.
